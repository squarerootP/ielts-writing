# OP3: prolonged overwork leads to heightened economic growth, but runs the risk of increased negative social consequences

---

Countries with longer average working time are more economically successful, but they are also likely to suffer from negative social consequences. To what extent do you agree with this statement?

---

**Model answer**:

It is often argued that countries where people work longer hours achieve stronger economic performance, although this may come at a considerable social cost. I largely agree with this view, since longer working time can increase total economic output, particularly in the short term, while excessive workloads can seriously damage family life and individual well-being.
On the one hand, longer working hours can contribute to economic growth by increasing the amount of labour available to businesses. In industries where output depends heavily on working time, employees who spend more hours at work may produce more goods or provide more services, increasing company revenues. These additional earnings can then support business expansion, investment and job creation. Employees may also gain practical experience more quickly when they spend more time performing work-related tasks. However, this benefit is not unlimited, as excessively long hours can eventually cause fatigue and reduce efficiency.
On the other hand, a culture of overwork can create serious social problems. Employees who regularly work long hours have less time for their partners, children and personal lives, which may encourage some people to postpone marriage or parenthood and thereby contribute to lower birth rates. Excessive workloads can also damage mental health. A persistent lack of sleep and leisure time can lead to chronic stress and burnout, while reducing opportunities to maintain friendships and family relationships. Consequently, the economic gains associated with longer working hours may be accompanied by substantial costs to social well-being.
In conclusion, I agree that longer working hours can contribute to economic success by increasing labour input and supporting business activity. Nevertheless, when working time becomes excessive, the resulting pressure on family life and mental health can produce serious social consequences.
